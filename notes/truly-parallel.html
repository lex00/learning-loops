Truly Parallel

A marble does nothing but compute. It never reaches a wait point, so nothing it does can let another marble run. Two lanes are two cores. Whether both lanes move, and whether anything can pull a marble off a lane, is the whole scene. Six variants, one track.

The lesson

Misconception it corrects. "Threads run my code in parallel." In Python threads the platform has two lanes and a padlock, and only the marble holding the padlock moves. Three marbles of pure work take ten ticks on two lanes, exactly what they take on one. Threads chop the work up. They do not make it faster.

What should be burned in after staring. Two lanes are two cores. The padlock decides how many marbles move at once, and the claw decides whether a marble that keeps running can be taken off its lane. Drop the padlock and both lanes move: ten ticks become six. Take away the claw and a marble keeps its lane until it is done, so the marble behind it waits the whole time.

Six runtimes, four schedules. Threads and asyncio tie at ten ticks, and that tie is the lesson: a padlock on two lanes is one lane. Free-threaded Python, multiprocessing, and Go share a schedule, two lanes and a claw. Tokio has the lanes and no claw, and pays one tick for it. Toggling threads to free-threaded is the beat that answers the misconception: the gate opens, the padlock vanishes, and the marble that was standing at the gate starts to move.

The skeleton and the beats

Each marble has the same authored skeleton: run three ticks of work, done. There is no trapdoor. The schedule is derived from that skeleton by the variant's rules: how many lanes the lift may fill, how many marbles may move at once, and whether the claw pulls a marble that has run two ticks straight when another marble is waiting behind it. The quantum is two ticks in every variant that has a claw, so the OS time slice, Python's switch interval, and Go's ten milliseconds are drawn alike. Three things fall out of the derivation. Threads and asyncio land on the same ten ticks by different routes, one chopped up and one whole, which is the honest picture of the GIL. The claw makes the platform fair, so every marble is moving by tick three, and here it also makes the cycle shorter, because a marble can give back a lane before it is done. And tokio's tail is a straggler: two marbles finish together and the third runs alone on a wide platform, which is what a CPU-bound task does to a work-stealing runtime with more tasks than workers.

Morph rules

Toggling a variant changes parameters, and the scene continues from its current state. Nothing is reset. Marbles keep their dots and their position.

  • Threads to free-threaded. The padlock vanishes, the gate opens, and the marble standing at it starts moving at once. Both lanes are lit. This is the toggle the scene is built around.
  • Free-threaded to threads. The padlock appears on the marble that has run longest. The other marble on the platform stops where it is and the gate closes behind it. It moves again when the padlock comes to it.
  • Any two-lane variant to asyncio. Lane two goes dashed. The marble on it rolls back to the front of the ramp, as in the base scene, the one moment a marble moves backward.
  • Asyncio to any two-lane variant. The lift raises the front marble onto lane two at once, to the gate under threads, moving under the rest.
  • Claw appears (tokio to Go, asyncio to threads). It is checked at the next quantum boundary. A marble that has already run two ticks with another waiting is pulled off then.
  • Claw vanishes (Go to tokio, threads to asyncio). Marbles on lanes keep them until done. The ramp waits.
  • Marble weight tweens, and words swap in place, as in the base scene.

Timing and rhythm

The beat

Same as the base scene. One tick is one held picture, about a second, then four tenths of a second of movement.

The cycle

Fourteen seconds under threads and asyncio, ten under tokio, eight and a half with a claw. The cycle length is itself the lesson, so the strip and the schedule panel should show it plainly.

The claw

Descends during the movement window of a quantum beat, closes on the marble, and carries it over the lift to the back of the ramp. It moves only when it takes something. It never descends for show.

The quantum

Two ticks everywhere. Real intervals differ by orders of magnitude, from Python's five milliseconds to a Linux time slice, and none of that is the lesson. The beat bar can tick twice per quantum so the switch is anticipated.

Deliberately left out

  • No trapdoor and no pockets. A marble here never needs a reply. The moment it would, we are back in Waiting on I/O.
  • No memory. Whether the two moving marbles share a heap is the sharing scene's question, with a box on the platform to fight over.
  • No start-up cost for processes. Starting a process means starting Python again, and it deserves a beat, but not here. Multiprocessing is drawn as free-threaded with a heavier marble.
  • No cooperative yield. A tokio task can call yield_now, an asyncio task can await sleep(0), and a Python thread can call time.sleep(0). Those are the running marble choosing to leave, and they belong to a later scene about being polite.
  • No code. The words are the vocabulary of the runtime.
  • No predict card yet. The natural place is tick three under threads: which marble moves now?

Calls made, and calls to make

  • The stalled thread stops behind a red bar that stands just ahead of it, wherever it is on the lane: at the lane start when it is fresh, at its mark when it came back from the ramp. This is the concept note's gate, drawn where the thread actually is, in view of the marble that passes.
  • A preempted marble goes back to the ramp, at the back, and keeps its progress. When the lift raises it again it rolls along the lane to the mark it had reached and carries on from there, which is what saving and restoring a thread's state looks like. The ramp already means runnable and waiting for a lane, which is what a preempted thread is.
  • The claw belongs to whoever the lift is. Under threads it is drawn on the lift and labelled with the switch interval. Under Go it is the runtime's. Under asyncio and tokio the hook is empty.
  • Python threads draw two lanes, not one. The lanes are cores, and the point is that the second core is there and unused. The slot count in the manifest becomes two numbers: lanes, and marbles that may move.
  • The manifest gains one field per variant, preempt, and one skeleton with no wait segment. The state machine is the base scene's with pockets removed and the claw rule added. The validators need one new cell state for a marble at the gate.
  • To decide: whether the claw is drawn taking the marble across the top and down the lift shaft, or simply lifting it and dropping it onto the ramp below. The first reads as the scheduler doing it. The second is quicker.
  • To decide: whether the switch interval should be a visible timer on the claw, counting two ticks, or left to the beat bar.
