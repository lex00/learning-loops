Base Scene: Giving Up Control

A marble reaches a point where it needs something it does not have yet, leaves the running platform, and another marble runs. Three variants, one track.

The lesson

Misconception it corrects. "My three tasks run at the same time." In asyncio and in Python threads, exactly one marble is ever on the platform. Progress comes from waits overlapping, never from runs overlapping. Go widens the platform, and even there marbles queue when the slots are full.

What should be burned in after staring. The platform is narrow. The pockets are where time passes. A marble that becomes ready does not run until a slot opens, and in Python nothing but the running marble can open it.

Threads and asyncio produce the same schedule in this scene. That is deliberate and true: for work that only waits on replies, one slot is one slot. What differs is who owns the lift, what the slot is called, and how heavy the marble is. The difference in behaviour arrives in scene two, when a marble refuses to yield.

The skeleton and the beats

Each marble has the same authored skeleton: run one tick to reach the trapdoor, wait two ticks for a reply, run two ticks to finish. The schedule is derived from that skeleton by the variant's rules: slot count, and lift moves only when a slot is free. Deriving it, rather than hand-writing three schedules, is what lets a toggle mid-run recompute the future from the current state without resetting.

Two things fall out of the derivation without being designed. Go finishes the cycle in eight ticks against ten, which you see as the loop simply coming round sooner. And Go has a beat where the whole machine is idle, because the waits happened to line up, which is the honest picture of an IO-bound program.

Morph rules

Toggling a variant changes parameters, and the scene continues from its current state. Nothing is reset. Marbles keep their dots and their position.

  • Slot count changes. Going to two slots, the lift immediately raises the front marble if one is runnable. Going to one slot, the marble in slot two rolls back to the front of the ramp. That is the one moment a marble moves backward, and it is labelled so.
  • Marble size tweens between heavy and light over one tick.
  • Words swap in place. If a marble is mid-drop through the trapdoor, the word changes mid-air.
  • Pocket timers keep counting. A reply is a reply in every runtime.
  • Slot and lift labels swap. The GIL becomes the loop becomes a P.
  • The other two variants' paths stay drawn and dim. Slot two is visible in every variant, dashed, so the toggle to Go is a slot filling in rather than appearing.

Timing and rhythm

The beat

One tick is one held picture. Hold for about a second, then roughly four tenths of a second of movement into the next. A caught frame is always a held state, never a marble mid-air, except during the movement window.

The cycle

About fourteen seconds in the Python variants and eleven in Go. Long enough to be a scene, short enough to be seen many times in a sitting.

The word

Appears next to the marble at the moment of the transition and fades over the following tick. It is the only text that moves.

Jitter

None in the proof of concept. Reply times are fixed so the loop is identical every cycle. Jitter is a later switch, and when it comes it belongs on the outside world, not the runtime.

Deliberately left out

  • No gate. All three variants can give up control at a wait point, so no marble stalls in this scene. The gate arrives in the parallelism scene, where a Python thread reaches CPU work and the GIL holds it.
  • No preemption. The lift has no timer here. Scene two adds a marble that never reaches the trapdoor and shows which lifts can pull it off anyway.
  • No code. The words are the vocabulary of the runtime, not syntax. A hover whisper with the actual line can come later.
  • No predict card yet. The natural place is tick five, asking whether the ready marble runs now. Add once the loop is watchable.

Calls made

  • The marble waits for a reply from the outside world, drawn as a box beside the pockets. A small hollow dot travels from the box to the pocket when the reply lands.
  • Thread marbles are drawn heavy. In this scene it changes nothing about the schedule, and that is the point: toggling threads to asyncio changes the words and the weight and nothing else.
  • Slot two is a dashed slot visible in every variant and solid in Go. The platform never widens; a path fills in.
